Significant Figures

1. The Rules of Significant Figures

Uncertain Digits

Rule: All certain digits + 1 estimated digit.

Graduated cylinder reads 2.3 - 2.4
Estimate: 2.35
The '5' is uncertain but significant.

Non-Zero Integers

  • Always count as significant.
1234 has 4 sig figs or infinite sigfigs (see Exact Numbers)
32.1 has 3 sig figs

Leading Zeros

  • Zeros that precede all non-zero digits.
  • Never significant. They just place the decimal.
0.0025 has 2 sig figs
0.416 has 3 sig figs

Captive Zeros

  • Zeros between non-zero digits.
  • Always significant.
1008 has 4 sig figs
5.02 has 3 sig figs

Trailing Zeros

  • Zeros at the right end of the number.
  • Significant ONLY if the number contains a decimal point.
100 has 1 sig fig (ambiguous, assume 1)
100. has 3 sig figs
100.0 has 4 sig figs
0.020 has 2 sig figs

Exact Numbers

  • Numbers determined by counting or specific definition.
  • Have an infinite number of sig figs.
Converting inches to cm (1 in = 2.54 cm).
Counting: 3 (this is countable).
Caution:

$$ 3 \times 54 = 162 $$ Here, 3 and 54 are exact counts of items. Result has infinite sig figs.

$$ 3 \times 5.3 = 20 $$ Here, 3 is multiplied by a precise measurement so it's treated as 1 sig fig. Result must be rounded to 1 sig fig: \( 15.9 \rightarrow 20 \)

2. Mathematical Operations

Multiplication & Division

The result holds the same number of significant figures as the measurement with the FEWEST significant figures.

$$ 4.56 \times 1.4 = 6.384 $$ Round to 2 sigs: 6.4

Addition & Subtraction

The result has the same number of decimal places as the measurement with the FEWEST decimal places.

$$ 12.11 + 18.0 + 1.013 = 31.123 $$ Limiting term: 18.0 (1 decimal place)
Round to 1 decimal: 31.1

3. Handling Intermediary Steps

In multistep calculations, do NOT round at every step. Instead, track the significant figures of each step and round only at the final answer.

Example

Problem: Calculate \( (2.34 \times 1.2) + 4.56 \) assuming strict sig fig rules.

Incorrect (Rounding too early)

Step 1: Multiply

$$ 2.34 \times 1.2 = 2.808 $$

Limiting sigs: 1.2 (2 sig figs). Round immediately.

$$ \text{Result} = 2.8 $$

Step 2: Add

$$ 2.8 + 4.56 = 7.36 $$

Limiting decimals: 2.8 (1 decimal place).

$$ \text{Final Answer} = \mathbf{7.4} $$
Correct (Tracking Sig Figs)

Step 1: Multiply

$$ 2.34 \times 1.2 = 2.808 $$

Limiting sigs: 1.2 (2 sig figs). Do NOT round yet. Track the last significant digit.

$$ \text{Tracked: } 2.\underline{8}08 $$

Step 2: Add

$$ 2.\underline{8}08 + 4.56 = 7.368 $$

Limiting rule: Addition relies on precision (decimal places).
\( 2.\underline{8}08 \) ends at the tenths place.
\( 4.56 \) ends at the hundredths place.

Round 7.368 to tenths place.

$$ \text{Final Answer} = \mathbf{7.4} $$

Divergent Example (Where it matters)

Problem: Perform \( 3.55 \times 3.3333 \) if the target is 3 significant figures.

Incorrect Intermediate Rounding
$$ 3.55 \times 3.3333 = 11.833215 $$ $$ \text{Round to 3 sigs (because of 3.55): } 11.8 $$ $$ \text{Next: } \frac{11.8}{2.056} = 5.739299... $$ $$ \text{Round to 3 sigs: } \mathbf{5.74} $$
Correct Retention
$$ 3.55 \times 3.3333 = 11.833215 $$ $$ \text{Track 3 sigs (because of 3.55): } 11.\underline{8}33215 $$ $$ \text{Next: } \frac{11.833215}{2.056} = 5.755454... $$ $$ \text{Round to 3 sigs: } \mathbf{5.76} $$
