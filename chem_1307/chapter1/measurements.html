Common Unit Conversions

These conversion factors are essential for chemistry calculations.

Length

  • 1 m = 100 cm (exact)
  • 1 m = 1000 mm (exact)
  • 1 km = 1000 m (exact)
  • 1 inch = 2.54 cm (exact)
  • 1 mile = 5280 ft (exact)
  • 1 yard = 3 ft (exact)
  • 1 mile ≈ 1.609 km

Volume

  • 1 L = 1000 mL (exact)
  • 1 L = 1 dm3 (exact)
  • 1 cm3 = 1 cc = 1 mL (exact)
  • 1 gallon ≈ 3.785 L
  • 1 quart ≈ 0.946 L
  • 1 fl oz ≈ 29.57 mL

Mass

  • 1 kg = 1000 g (exact)
  • 1 g = 1000 mg (exact)
  • 1 metric ton = 1000 kg (exact)
  • 1 lb = 16 oz (exact)
  • 1 lb ≈ 453.6 g
  • 1 kg ≈ 2.2046 lb

Note on Exact Numbers

Values marked with (exact) are defined values. They have an infinite number of significant figures.

When performing calculations, exact numbers do not limit the precision of your result. Only approximate measurements affect the significant figures of your final answer.

Reading the Meniscus

When measuring liquids in graduated cylinders, always read at the bottom of the meniscus (the curved surface).

What is a Meniscus?

A meniscus is the curve seen at the top of a liquid in response to its container. Most liquids (like water) form a concave meniscus that curves downward.

Always read at eye level at the bottom of the curve

Proper Technique

  1. Eye Level: Position your eyes at the same level as the meniscus to avoid parallax error
  2. Bottom of Curve: Read the measurement at the lowest point of the meniscus
  3. Estimate: Record one digit beyond the smallest graduation (the uncertain digit)
  4. Lighting: Ensure good lighting to clearly see the meniscus
Common Mistakes
  • Reading from above or below (parallax error)
  • Reading the top of the meniscus instead of the bottom
  • Not estimating the uncertain digit

Uncertainty & Precision

All measurements have some degree of uncertainty. The last digit recorded is always an estimate.

The Uncertain Digit

When using any measuring device, you should record all certain digits plus one estimated (uncertain) digit.

Example: Ruler Measurement

A ruler marked in centimeters with millimeter subdivisions:

  • Certain digits: Can be read directly from markings
  • Uncertain digit: Estimated between the smallest markings

If an object falls between 2.3 cm and 2.4 cm, you might record 2.37 cm
The '7' is your estimated digit

Precision vs Accuracy

Precision

How close measurements are to each other

Reproducibility

Accuracy

How close a measurement is to the true value

Correctness

Ideal: High precision AND high accuracy

Measurements cluster tightly around the true value

Significant Figures in Measurements

The number of significant figures indicates the precision of a measurement.

More Precise Instrument

12.45 g

Less Precise Instrument

12 g

Recording Measurements

  1. Read all certain digits from the scale
  2. Estimate one more digit between the smallest divisions
  3. Record the measurement with the proper number of significant figures
  4. Include units with every measurement

Remember: Never record more digits than your instrument can measure!
