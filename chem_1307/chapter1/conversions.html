Dimensional Analysis

Mastering unit conversions with the "Railroad Track" method

The Base Unit Method

When converting between two units that both have prefixes (like converting from micrometers to petameters), always convert to the base unit first.

The Golden Rule

When writing equivalence statements for prefixes:

1. The "1" always goes with the modified unit (the one with the prefix).

2. The Power of 10 always goes with the base unit.

Example: Micro-

prefix: micro- ($\mu$), factor: $10^{-6}$

1 $\mu m$ = $10^{-6}$ $m$

Two-Step Conversion Strategy

To convert from unit A to unit B:

  1. Convert Unit A $\rightarrow$ Base Unit
  2. Convert Base Unit $\rightarrow$ Unit B
Example: Convert $\mu m$ to $Pm$
$\mu m$ → $m$ → $Pm$

Dimensional Analysis ("Railroad Tracks")

Using a grid (or "railroad tracks") ensures your units cancel out correctly. Multiply numbers on the top, divide by numbers on the bottom.

Visual Guide

Example: Convert $4.5 \times 10^2$ $\mu m$ to $Pm$

$$ 4.5 \times 10^2 \text{ }\mu m \times \frac{10^{-6} \text{ } m}{1 \text{ } \mu m} \times \frac{1 \text{ } Pm}{10^{15} \text{ } m} = 4.5 \times 10^{-19} \text{ } Pm $$

Note how $\mu m$ cancels with $\mu m$, and $m$ cancels with $m$, leaving only $Pm$.
